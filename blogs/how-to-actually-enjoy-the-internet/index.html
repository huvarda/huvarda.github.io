How to Actually Enjoy the Internet

Here are a collection of tips to get enjoyment out of the internet.

get an adblocker

A huge chunk of the problems with the modern internet stem from one thing: advertising. Why do companies collect so much data on you? Advertising. Why does social media promote content designed to make you angry? So you stay longer for them to be able to advertise more to you. The best way to fight back against this is to minimize the amount of advertising you see.

Some morons may claim that using an adblocker harms the people producing content. If you really care that much about the wellbeing of your favorite youtubers, consider donating to their Patreon/Kofi/whatever. A one-time donation of $5 is far more than what they would have gotten from you had you watched every advertisement on every video they upload for years, so don’t waste your time.

It is your duty, not just to use adblockers, but to get everyone you know to use them as well. Whenever I’m at a friend or relative’s house and I notice they aren’t using an adblocker, or they are setting up a new device, I make sure to install uBlock Origin for them. If everyone did this the world would be a better place, I’m sure.

explore

Stop endlessly scrooolling through the same 5 social media websites.

curate

When you do use a mainstream social media website try to use the algorithmic feed as little as possible. The algorithm isn’t designed to enrich your experience, it’s designed to make you stay on the website as long as possible. That does mean that sometimes you will be served a piece of content that you really like, but more often than not you’ll be given a helping of lowest-common-denominator slop and ragebait.

join sub-communities

A. This is bo. If you generously presumed that a random person has a 1% chance of being horrible, that means an online community of 1,000,000 people would have ~1,000 insufferable members. Not only that, but these 1%ers are more likely to be vocal which skews your perception of the community as a whole.

know your bait

There are 3 main types of bait: engagement bait, sympathy bait, and ragebait. I’ll briefly touch on the first 2 and go into more detail on ragebait since that’s the kind you’ll most frequently encounter (and the kind that will negatively affect your internet experience the most)

engagement bait

I guess you could technically call all bait “engagement bait” but here I’m referring to content that is completely devoid of substance and only exists for engagement. Some examples of this include

  • What do these colors remind you of?
  • Type ‘x’ with your eyes closed.
  • [Character with gun] Reply with your most recent saved image

Engagement bait shouldn’t be confused for just prompting discussion. I’ve seen people reply to posts such as “What are your thoughts on this episode of [show]?” with “engagement bait” when it doesn’t necessarily have to be. An easy litmus test for engagement bait is whether or not the author of the post is participating in the discussion beyond a surface level. If so, it’s not bait.

sympathy bait

Bait that exploits peoples’ sympathy for post interactions. Extra points if there is no call to action beyond “like and share.”

ragebait

This is the form of bait you’ll most frequently come across, and it’s also the kind that will ruin your internet experience the most, which is why it’s important to be able to know how to identify it, and how to deal with it.

Trolls aren’t obligated to break character when asked. It’s astonishing the number of times I’ve seen people declare obvious trolling to be reflective of genuine because the troll said they weren’t trolling

The healthiest mindset to have with ragebait is something along the lines of “This person most likely doesn’t believe this, they are just saying inflammatory things to get interactions. If they do genuinely believe this, no amount of me insulting or attempting to refute them is going to change their mind.” With ragebait, the block button (or mute button if that also exists) is your best friend.

insta-blocks

There are a handful of indicators that a post will likely piss you off before even consuming the content of it. Here’s a collection:

  • Any street interview clip
  • Any “grindset” podcast clip
  • Anything that mentions a currently relevant political issue-even if I agree with it

how to tell ragebait apart from jokes

One frustrating thing I see is people replying to obvious jokes with “bad bait”. Try asking yourself “If some hypothetical person believed this, would it be really funny, or would it be angering?”
