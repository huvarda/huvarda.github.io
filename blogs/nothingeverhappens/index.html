Nothing Ever Happens?

By now you’ve probably heard the phrase ‘Nothing Ever Happens’ somewhere online, usually in response to news or just as a joke-y nonsequitor. But what does it actually refer to? In this post I’m going to go into how I interpret the phrase, and how much I agree with it.

The main way I see the phrase used is as an indictment of the current clickbait landscape we live in. Every piece of “news”, regardless of severity (or even the source being trustworthy at all) being peddled as “the big new thing to worry about/be excited for” has worn a lot of people out. “New strain of flu discovered in China”? We’ve had “the new COVID” announced every 2 weeks for the past few years now. Nothing ever happens. “New cure for cancer found”? Nah, nothing ever happens. Will Half Life 3 be announced at The Game Awards? Of course not, nothing ever happens.

How agreeable this is of course depends on the trustworthiness of the source but in general I do think it’s nice that healthy skepticism against clickbait headlines is a bit more common (even if some do take it too far and outright reject all news.)

The other way I see the phrase being used is kind of like more cynical version of the quote “History may not repeat itself, but it often rhymes.” “Nothing ever happens” doesn’t literally mean that nothing happens. I mean things clearly happen all the time! But even when “big things” happen it feels like they just don’t have consequences anymore, rather they’re forgotten about in a week.

With this framing the phrase is something more like “[In the broad context of society] nothing [extremely good or bad] ever happens [with lasting consequences] [but society as a whole is still getting worse in small incremental ways that we can’t do anything about because the institutions that would once be used to uphold social order now have far more friction when trying to improve things compared to making them worse]”

On one hand this line of thinking is almost comforting in a way. Everything is slowly getting worse, sure, but at the end of the day there’s nothing you can do about it.

A while back I got recommended an old JibJab animation about the political landscape in 2005 and it was shockingly relevant to today. In fact basically every issue mentioned in the video has persisted to today, and now on top of those we have a whole new slew of issues.
